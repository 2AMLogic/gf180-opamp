## Method

- Input: VCM - 50 mV until 100 ns, a 1 ns rising edge to VCM + 50 mV, a falling edge back at 600 ns, run to 1100 ns; `.tran 1e-09 1.1e-06` (ngspice's internal step is bounded by the same 1 ns).
- **Window vs small-signal GBW**: the ratified GBW >= 10 MHz gives a closed-loop time constant tau <= 1/(2 pi GBW) = 15.9 ns; each edge window is 500 ns = 31 tau (a single-pole response reaches 0.1 % in 6.9 tau = 110 ns). The settled level is the time-averaged output over the last 50 ns of each window (ending 10 ns before the next edge, or at the end of the run).
- **Levels**: v0 = vout 10 ns before the edge; vfinal = reference-window time average. Both must equal vinp to 20 mV and the realised step must be 100 mV +-20%, else the point is INVALID. y = (vout - v0) / (vfinal - v0).
- **Overshoot** = max(y) - 1 (%, 0 if never above); **preshoot** = -min(y) (wrong-way start). **Monotonic** iff the largest fall of y below its running maximum and the preshoot are both <= 0.1% of the step.
- **Settling time** to band b = time from the input edge (start of the 1 ns ramp) to the last exit of |y - 1| from b, interpolated linearly; **not settled** if the output is still outside b inside the reference window, or drifts by more than 0.1 x b across it (a slow tail hidden in the window average); reported as such and counted as the worst settling result. Largest reference-window drift over the grid: 3.4e-08 % of the step.
- **10-90 % time** is reported only to show how far a 100 mV step is from small-signal behaviour.
- Per point the reported overshoot and settling times are the WORSE of the rising and falling edges; the per-edge values are in the table.
